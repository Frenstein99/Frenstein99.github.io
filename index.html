Thesis

Imprints of Dynamical Dark Energy Models on Observables of Large-Scale Structures: from CMB to Lensing Spectra

  • Supervisor: Pier-Stefano CORASANITI, Research Director, CNRS, Paris Observatory-PSL
  • Thesis Duration: 6 months (March 2025 – September 2025)
  • Tools: Python, CLASS, Cobaya (Bayesian analysis), Planck data
  • Location: Observatoire de Paris-PSL, Meudon Campus, 92195 Meudon, France

Phenomenological Approach to Dark Energy

In this part of my thesis, I explore dark energy from a phenomenological perspective by focusing on the evolution of its equation of state across cosmic time. I examine widely used parametrizations, such as the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) form and kink models, which allow for dynamical departures from a cosmological constant. Different classes of scalar field models (freezing, thawing, and phantom scenarios) are discussed to highlight their distinct physical behaviors. I study the cosmological evolution of kink models as a function of the scale factor and connect phenomenological descriptions to fundamental theory by fitting the equation of state w(a) obtained from scalar field solutions of the Klein–Gordon equation for several physically motivated potentials. CPL fails to capture rapid scalar field variations unlike kink parametrizations, which fit both rapidly and slowly varying scalar potentials smoothly.

Limits of the CPL Parametrization

This chapter critically examines the ability of the CPL parametrization to capture rapidly and slowly varying dark energy dynamics using fiducial kink models. CPL exhibits systematic bias when applied to dynamical dark energy models, particularly in the phantom regime, and fails to accurately capture rapid variations in the equation of state, while providing a reasonably good fit for slowly evolving dark energy scenarios.

Cosmological Imprints of the Kink Model

This chapter explores how kink models of dark energy leave distinct signatures on cosmological observables. I analyze the deceleration parameter and study the resulting effects on the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) spectrum, distinguishing between slowly and rapidly varying models. Special attention is given to the Integrated Sachs–Wolfe (ISW) effect, which highlights the impact of dark energy dynamics on the growth of cosmic structures and large-scale temperature fluctuations.

Planck Data Analysis

In this chapter, I analyze the latest Planck CMB data to extract cosmological information and constrain dark energy models. Using Cobaya (Bayesian Likelihood Inference), I explored Planck+Kink likelihoods where both rapidly and slowly varying kink models are very consistent with the standard cosmological constant.
